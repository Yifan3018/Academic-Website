Working Papers

Space versus Scope: Firm Heterogeneity under Domestic Trade BarriersMarch 2026

Third-year paper

Domestic trade barriers in India have been shown to impede commodity flows across states. This study examines how manufacturing plants optimally choose the number of products to produce and the number of markets to serve. The tension between these dimensions is suggested by two data patterns: (1) the share of single-product plants rises as distribution expenses decline, and (2) distribution expenses are highest for single-product plants, decrease with broader product scope, and then increase again once scope becomes sufficiently large. To rationalize these observations, I develop a model in which a plant allocates limited managerial effort across products, creating a trade-off between product scope and spatial market expansion. The strength of this trade-off depends on the relative magnitudes of fixed costs in market entry and product management, together with variable distribution costs.

Defensive Expenditures against Air Pollution in TaiwanFebruary 2023

Master's thesis · Advisors: Prof. Jin-Tan Liu & Prof. Chun-Fang Chiang

This study characterizes defensive expenditures targeting air pollution in Taiwan using two data sets: electronic invoice data and experimental data. It first investigates how individuals respond to air pollution by purchasing face masks, applying a random utility model to estimate the marginal willingness to pay (MWTP) for clean air from consumption records, then analyzes a field experiment to estimate MWTP from subjects' responses. The evidence shows that (1) Taiwanese residents responded to air pollution daily in a timely manner; (2) city residents, wealthier residents, and more educated individuals had higher MWTP relative to other groups; (3) the estimated daily MWTP for removing a unit of PM2.5 is 0.266 or 0.353 (NTD) for the overall Taiwanese population, depending on approach.
