About

I am a PhD student in Economics at the Pennsylvania State University. My research interests lie in international trade, economic development, and firm dynamics — in particular, how firms adjust the scope and geography of their operations in response to frictions such as domestic trade barriers.

Before Penn State, I completed an M.A. in Economics at National Taiwan University, and spent a year as a visiting student in the Department of Economics at UC Berkeley. I hold a B.A. in Economics (minor in Sociology), also from National Taiwan University.

My third-year paper, "Space versus Scope: Firm Heterogeneity under Domestic Trade Barriers," studies how manufacturing plants in India trade off product scope against geographic market reach when facing costly domestic distribution. See the Research page for more, and the CV page for full details on publications, teaching, and RA experience.
